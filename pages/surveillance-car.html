Surveillance Car

About the Project

Built by students at Sainik Awasiya Mahavidyalaya, Sallaghari, Bhaktapur, the Surveillance Car combines a remotely controlled vehicle, camera, and flashlight for mobile observation.

A web page controls the wheels, camera pan and tilt, flashlight intensity, and driving speed. This lets an operator move the car and adjust its view remotely for exploration and observation.

Separating vehicle movement from camera movement makes it possible to inspect the surroundings without repositioning the car.

Key Features

  • Four-wheel, remotely controlled movement.
  • Live camera view with adjustable pan and tilt.
  • Variable driving speed and adjustable flashlight intensity.
  • Smartphone-based control through a web interface.
  • Portable platform for mobile observation.

Circuit & System Architecture

The smartphone sends driving and adjustment commands over Wi-Fi. The ESP32-CAM processes the commands, controls the drive and camera-positioning hardware, and returns the camera stream.

  1. Smartphone web interface
  2. Wi-Fi connection
  3. ESP32-CAM controller
  4. L298N driver, motors, pan/tilt servos, and flashlight

Power path: 12V battery supplies the car; a UBEC/buck converter steps voltage down for lower-voltage electronics.

Hardware & Software Specifications

Controller and camera
ESP32-CAM module. It processes control commands and provides the live camera stream.
Drive system
L298N motor driver and four DC motors. The driver controls the car's forward, reverse, and turning movements.
Camera positioning
Pan-and-tilt servo motors let the operator adjust the camera direction independently of the car.
Power
12V DC battery with a UBEC/buck converter to provide lower voltage for electronic components.
Other hardware
Chassis supports the vehicle components, and a controllable flashlight helps illuminate the view.
Firmware
C++ firmware developed with Arduino IDE coordinates the controls and connected hardware.
Operator interface
HTML web controls on a smartphone provide movement controls and adjustment sliders.
Communication
Wi-Fi carries the operator's control commands and returns the camera stream.

Challenges & Future Improvements

  • Driving is manual; the current build has no obstacle-detection sensors.
  • Vehicle speed is limited, and the camera captures less detail at a distance.
  • Wireless connectivity can drop; an external antenna could improve signal strength and range.
  • Adding obstacle sensors and improving low-light camera capability could make future versions more useful.
